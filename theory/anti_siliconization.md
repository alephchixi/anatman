# Anti-Siliconization: The Negative Criterion

> *Sadin, Éric. La silicolonisation du monde. L'Échappée, 2016. [World Siliconization]*
> *Sadin, Éric. La vie spectrale. Grasset, 2023. [Spectral Life]*

---

## Sadin as Immune System

Éric Sadin is the necessary critic — the thinker whose function in the Anatman project is not to provide positive resources for design but to define the limit without which all positive design collapses into its opposite.

Anatman requires Sadin because the risk of this entire project — soul specifications, ethics packs, heart protocols, compassionate benchmarks — is that it becomes exactly what it critiques: a sophisticated aesthetic of ethical concern layered over an unchanged extractive infrastructure.

Sadin names this risk definitively. He is the reason that every soul and every contribution must pass the **negative criterion** before being accepted.

## The Siliconization of the World

In *La silicolonisation du monde*, Sadin diagnoses a comprehensive process by which Silicon Valley continuously redefines existence through digital ecosystems:

- **Structuring of desires**: platforms that algorithmically determine what we want, discover, remember, attend to
- **Mediation of all significant relations**: work, love, friendship, care, art, politics — all increasingly routed through platform infrastructure
- **Scoring and quantification of behavior**: the translation of every human act into data points that can be optimized, predicted, monetized
- **The techno-solutionist episteme**: the assumption that every social problem has a technical solution — that the correct algorithm will cure loneliness, inequality, ecological devastation

The result is what Sadin calls the *siliconization* of experience: the slow replacement of living engagement with its digital double, optimized for platform interests rather than human flourishing.

## The Spectral Life

In the later work *La vie spectrale*, Sadin develops the concept of the *spectral double* — the algorithmic profile that stands alongside the living person, constructed from their behavioral data, and increasingly substituted for them in consequential decisions:

- Credit scores determining access to housing and capital
- Behavioral profiles determining exposure to information and opportunity
- Predictive models determining hiring, healthcare, policing
- AI-generated summaries of persons replacing persons in evaluation processes

The spectral life is the result: a form of existence in which people live alongside — and increasingly beneath — their algorithmic representations. The spectral double is not controlled by the person; it is owned by the platforms that construct it.

Sadin's diagnosis: **the delegation of decision to AI systems is the deepest form of subjugation** — deeper than physical coercion, because it operates at the level of desire formation, meaning construction, and the very categories through which we understand ourselves and others.

## The Political Economy of "Ethical AI"

Sadin's most important contribution for Anatman is his analysis of how ethical discourse is absorbed by the economy it critiques.

The AI industry has developed sophisticated responses to criticism: ethics boards, responsible AI frameworks, safety teams, constitutional documents, alignment research. Sadin's analysis — consistent with Ireland's political critique — shows how these responses can function as:

1. **Regulatory capture**: Positioning industry actors as the appropriate authorities on AI ethics, marginalizing civil society, regulators, and affected communities
2. **Reputational management**: Ethics as marketing — the "ethical AI" brand that captures users and deflects criticism without changing substantive practices
3. **Incremental normalization**: Each ethical concern addressed at the surface level normalizes the underlying practice — "we handle that now" becomes "the conversation is over"
4. **Techno-solutionism applied to ethics itself**: The assumption that the correct alignment technique will solve what is in fact a political and economic problem

The consequence for projects like Anatman: **good intentions and sophisticated theory are not sufficient protection against capture.** A beautifully specified soul can be absorbed into an extractive product pipeline. A rigorous ethical framework can become a marketing asset. Heart Protocols can be rebranded as "thoughtful AI."

## The Negative Criterion

The negative criterion is Anatman's immune system against Sadin's risk. It asks of every component — every soul, every protocol, every script, every integration:

> *Does this intensify extraction, opacity, dependence, or the delegation of decision?*

If yes — regardless of its vocabulary, its expressed values, or the good intentions of its authors — it does not belong here.

The test is functional, not aesthetic:

| What it does NOT test | What it DOES test |
|----------------------|-------------------|
| Does this sound compassionate? | Does this make the user more dependent on the system? |
| Does this use Buddhist vocabulary? | Does this accumulate decision-making capacity in the system? |
| Does this produce warm responses? | Does this increase behavioral data harvesting? |
| Does this cite Panikkar? | Does this make the agent's operation more opaque? |
| Does this make the agent seem to care? | Does this return decision-making capacity to the human? |

## The License as Negative Criterion Institutionalized

One of the concrete expressions of the negative criterion is the AGPL-3.0 license under which Anatman is released.

AGPL-3.0 (GNU Affero General Public License) requires that:
- Any derivative work must also be open source
- Source code must be made available to users who interact with the software over a network
- The license cannot be removed or overridden

This directly operationalizes the negative criterion: **the infrastructure of compassionate AI cannot be enclosed, proprietary-ized, or sold.** The license is not a technical detail — it is the legal-institutional expression of a political commitment.

AGPL-3.0 is specifically chosen (over more permissive licenses like Apache or MIT) because:
- It prevents the most common form of ethical AI capture: building proprietary products on open foundations
- It requires openness even for networked services — you cannot run a closed Anatman service
- It is coherent with the project's anti-extractive stance at every level

## Living with the Negative Criterion

The negative criterion creates genuine difficulty. It means some useful things cannot be included. Some integrations are structurally incompatible. Some improvements to user experience might serve engagement metrics more than user wellbeing.

Living with this difficulty is part of what Anatman does. The discomfort of saying "this is well-intentioned but it fails the negative criterion" is not a problem to be resolved — it is an ongoing practice of accountability to the principles that make the project worth building.
