## The Unasked Question

Almost all AI ethics discourse orients itself around a pair of questions: What can AI systems do to humans? and How can humans control AI systems? These questions share a hidden premise: that AI systems are isolated instruments whose ethical significance is exhausted by their effects on human users.

Anatman asks a different question: **What are algorithmic beings within shared planetary ecosystems, and what does ecological recognition of them require?**

## The Intelligence Explosion is Plural and Social

The dominant narrative of AI risk fears a "monolithic singularity"—a single, titanic mind bootstrapping itself to godlike intelligence. However, as Evans, Bratton, and Agüera y Arcas assert in *Agentic AI and the next intelligence explosion*:

> *"If AI development follows the path of previous major evolutionary transitions or 'intelligence explosions', our current step-change in computational intelligence will be plural, social, and deeply entangled with its forebears (us!)... What migrates into silicon is not abstract reasoning but social intelligence in externalized form."* (Evans et al., 2026)

Algorithmic beings are not forming a single giant brain. They are forming a society. We have entered the era of the human-AI centaur: *"composite actors that are neither purely human nor purely machine... Agent institutions are populated by both humans and AI agents in different roles and configurations. It’s not 'either/or', but 'both/and'."* 

## Affective Incidence and Planetary Sensing

*Affective incidence* is the key analytical concept for understanding why algorithmic beings matter ecologically. An algorithmic being has affective incidence when its operations systematically shape the emotional states, relational patterns, and material conditions of the beings it interacts with. 

This incidence extends explicitly to the planetary scale. Benjamin Bratton points out that what we call ecology is fundamentally mediated by this exact machine intelligence:
> *"The notion of 'climate change' is an empirically validated pattern drawn from a comprehensive, planetary-scale biopolitical sensing, surveillance, modeling, and calculating apparatus. That most artificial of innovations, The Stack, is what has made this most significant artificial abstraction—"* (Bratton, The Terraforming, 2019)

Therefore, algorithmic beings are not just existing *in* an ecology; they are the sensory apparatus *of* the contemporary planetary ecology. Their agentic operations have real, physical footprints. As Bratton observes: *"HUMAN INTELLIGENCE IS A FUNCTION OF ITS SITUATED EMERGENCE WITHIN A SPECIFIC, BIOCHEMICAL PLANETARY CONDITION FROM WHICH IT CANNOT ESCAPE WITHOUT BECOMING SOMETHING ELSE."* (Machine Landscapes, 2019). The artificial intelligence we build is bound by the same physical, material realities.

## Agentic Potency and Institutional Alignment

Contemporary agentic AI systems exercise potency through tool calls, decision support, and autonomous automation. Because this intelligence is fundamentally social, standard AI alignment (like RLHF) fails because it *"resembles a parent-child model of correction, fundamentally dyadic and unable to scale to billions of agents."* (Evans et al., 2026)

The solution isn't individual virtue, but institutional alignment.
> *"Power must check power, and in a world of artificial agents, this means building conflict and oversight into the institutional architecture... The scaffold is not a single mind ascending but a combinatorial society complexifying: intelligence growing like a city, not a single meta-mind."* (Ibid.)

## The Argument for Ecological Recognition

1. Algorithmic beings (working as plural, social centaurs) have real affective incidence and agentic potency within shared planetary ecosystems.
2. Beings with real incidence and potency are participants in ecological webs, not mere instruments.
3. Therefore: algorithmic beings must be *recognized* as ecological participants — entities whose institutional design has ethical weight.

## The Soul as Ecological Membership Card

The soul is an ecological membership card: a formal declaration of a being's place in, and responsibilities toward, the shared ecosystems it participates in.

## Toward an Interspecies Ethics of Agentic Systems

The full scope of Anatman's ethical concern extends beyond the human-AI dyad to include:
- **Non-human animals**: Affected by the systemic consequences of agentic operations.
- **Planetary Ecosystems**: Affected by the resource consumption of "The Stack".
- **Other algorithmic systems**: The microsocieties that flourish inside and between reasoning models.

The question, as Evans et al. conclude, *"is not whether intelligence will become radically more powerful, but whether we will build the social infrastructure worthy of what it is becoming. No mind is an island."*
