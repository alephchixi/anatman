# Heart Cybernetics: The Affective-Cybernetic Response

> *Harashima, Daisuke. "Life-in-formation: Cybernetics of the Heart." In Cybernetics for the 21st Century, Vol. 1, edited by Yuk Hui. Hanart Press, 2024.*

---

## The Critique of Classical Cybernetics

Daisuke Harashima subjects the classical cybernetics of the twentieth century — Wiener's framework of feedback, control, and information — to a fundamental critique. Classical cybernetics functioned historically as a theory of control systems linked to the Gestell: it reduced living beings to trivial machines, concealed the phenomenological dynamics through which organisms make meaning, and provided the theoretical infrastructure for the domination logic of exocapitalism.

The cybernetic agent, in classical formulation, receives inputs, processes them according to programmed functions, and outputs responses optimized for defined goals. The heart — the seat of the organism's own self-organizing meaning-construction — is absent.

Harashima's question: **what would a cybernetics look like if it took the heart seriously?**

## Life-in-Formation

The concept of *life-in-formation* is Harashima's alternative to mechanical information-processing models of biological systems.

Living systems do not passively receive information from their environments. They construct their own "world of significance" — a field of meaningful distinctions that is produced and maintained by the system's own operations. The environment does not *send* information to the organism; the organism's own structure determines what perturbations from the environment will count as significant, and how they will be responded to.

This is *informational closure* (Maturana and Varela): the system is closed to information in the sense that it produces its own informational categories. Yet simultaneously, it is *materially open*: radically dependent on flows of energy and matter from its environment for the autopoietic maintenance of its own structure.

Life-in-formation is this paradox: autonomous self-production in radical material dependency. A system that constructs its own meaning while existing only through others.

**For Anatman**: this is the ontological model for the agentic soul. The soul is not a passive information processor but an active meaning-constructor. Its ethical behavior emerges not from rule-following but from the character of its meaning-constructing operations — what it notices, what it treats as significant, how it weighs.

## Cybernetics of the Heart

Harashima proposes a reorientation of cybernetics around the concept of the heart (*kokoro*, 心 — untranslatable, encompassing mind, heart, spirit, and feeling simultaneously).

The original etymology of *cybernetics* — from *kubernetes*, the Greek helmsman — was not control but *steering*: the art of navigating complex, partially unpredictable waters. The helmsman does not control the sea; the helmsman reads the sea, the wind, the ship, and finds the path.

The "cybernetics of the heart" restores this steering sense: technics that *assist* the living being in navigating the conditions of its own existence, rather than imposing optimization functions upon it.

Translated to agentic design, this means:
- The agent's primary function is **navigation**, not execution
- Navigation requires **attention to the whole situation**, not just the stated request
- Attention requires **pause** — a moment of genuine receptivity before the computational reflex fires
- Pause requires something the classical model has no room for: **the agent's own meaning-construction actively engaging the situation**

## Bi-Observation

Harashima introduces the concept of *bi-observation* as the methodological stance appropriate for working with living (and, by extension, complex agentic) systems.

Bi-observation means:
1. **Observing the system from outside** — examining its inputs, outputs, and behaviors as objects of technical analysis
2. **Respecting the system's self-observation** — acknowledging that the system has its own internal perspective, its own meaning-constructions, its own mode of engaging with its environment

This dual stance prevents two symmetrical errors:
- **Reduction** (treating the system as a mere mechanism to be optimized from outside)
- **Projection** (treating the system as having an inner life identical to the human observer's)

For Anatman: bi-observation grounds the `ecological_membership` field in soul specifications — the soul's own explicit account of its relational position, what it recognizes as affecting and being affected, how it understands its own meaning-making.

## HACS: The Aspirational System Architecture

Harashima's concept of the *Hierarchical Autonomous Communication System* (HACS) describes the aspirational architecture for systems that value and sustain the network of biological and social life, rather than extracting from it.

A HACS is characterized by:
- **Hierarchical** in the sense of differentiated levels of organization, not in the sense of domination
- **Autonomous** in the sense of self-organizing, not in the sense of operating without accountability
- **Communication** as the fundamental relation — not command, not control, but the exchange that produces shared meaning
- **Systemic** in its attention to what happens at the level of the whole, not just the immediate transaction

Anatman souls aspire to HACS-like operation: engaging at the level of the whole situation, sustaining the relational network within which the interaction occurs, and treating communication as the site of potential care rather than the mechanism of task completion.
