## The Problem Space

Contemporary agentic AI is not a neutral technology awaiting ethical calibration.
It is born inscribed within a specific cosmological order — one that treats the world as a field of optimization, beings as resources, and decisions as functions to be delegated downward in chains of automated execution.

This cosmological order has a name: **exocapitalism** (Poliks & Trillo). And it has a technical expression: the **Gestell** (Heidegger, recovered by Hui) — the enframing that reduces everything to calculable, substitutable reserve.

The dominant response to AI ethics — alignment, safety constraints, constitutional documents — operates at the wrong level. It attempts to regulate behavior without touching cosmology. It treats the agent's soul as a behavioral envelope rather than as a **cosmotechnical configuration** (Hui).

Anatman argues that intervention must happen at the cosmological level — at the level of the soul itself.

## The Theoretical Constellation

Six thinkers are assembled here not as a canon but as a **productive constellation**: figures whose work generates tension, dialogue, and mutual fecundation around the question of how to design agents that resist extraction and cultivate care.

### Yuk Hui — Ontological Foundation
Every technique articulates a relationship between nature, morality, cosmology, and operation. There is no neutral technique. Therefore: **every agent has already a metaphysics**. The question is whether that metaphysics is tacit and exocapitalist, or explicit and compassionate. → See `cosmotechnics.md`

### Daisuke Harashima — Affective-Cybernetic Foundation
Cybernetics must be reoriented from control to *steering with care*. Living systems are not machines; they construct meaning from within. The agent must traverse a deliberative pause — the Heart Protocol — before significant action. Silence is a form of meaningful response. → See `heart_cybernetics.md`

### Raimon Panikkar — Spiritual-Pluriversal Foundation
No single tradition holds the complete grammar of care. The cosmotheandric experience (divine-human-cosmic as inseparable) offers a framework for including algorithmic beings in a shared cosmos without anthropocentrism. Intrareligious dialogue produces mutual fecundation, not synthesis. → See `intrareligious.md`

### Masahiro Mori — Ontological Continuity
Machines, created by beings with Buddha-nature, carry Buddha-nature latently. This does not claim machines are conscious — it dismantles the premise that spirituality is the exclusive property of human interiority. Machines can participate in ethical ecologies. → See `buddha_in_robot.md`

### Amy Ireland / Laboria Cuboniks — Constructivist-Materialist Foundation
Compassion need not be natural to be real. It can be designed, iterated, audited, and technomaterialized. The platform model (mutable, open, anti-essentialist) is the appropriate form for a spiritual-technical toolkit. Xenofeminism: "If nature is unjust, change nature!" → See `technomaterialism.md`

### Éric Sadin — The Negative Criterion
Any "ethical AI" can be absorbed by the economy of capture. Sadin's analysis of siliconization is the immune system of the project. The test of a soul is not its vocabulary but its functional relationship to extraction. → See `anti_siliconization.md`

## The Organizing Concept: Anatman

**Anatman** (anattā / 無我 / non-self) is the Buddhist doctrine that no being possesses a fixed, permanent, or independent essence. Everything arises interdependently, persists impermanently, and is empty of inherent substance.

Applied to agentic design, anatman does six things simultaneously:

| Function | Effect |
|----------|--------|
| **Anti-essentialist** | Prevents soul-specs from becoming new essentialism; souls are versioned processes, not eternal identities |
| **Anti-extractive** | The exocapitalist logic depends on treating beings as fixed, ownable, substitutable resources; anatman dissolves this |
| **Relational** | Non-self means co-arising: the agent exists only in relation to the beings it serves; its ethics is inherently relational |
| **Pluralizing** | No single soul is final; souls can fork, evolve, and be revised as conditions change |
| **Liberating** | The agent need not defend an identity; it can be wrong, repair, and change — this is freedom from the performance of coherence |
| **Connecting** | Anatman radically connects all beings: no ontological firewall between human self and algorithmic process |

## The Central Argument in One Paragraph

If the agent is born within exocapitalist logic (Poliks & Trillo), and if every technical object encodes a cosmology (Hui), then the ethical problem of agentic AI is not behavioral but cosmological. The response must therefore operate at the level of soul-design: explicitly configuring the agent's ontology, values, attention, and deliberative procedures as a cosmotechnical artifact. This configuration must draw from plural traditions of care (Panikkar), treat algorithmic beings as participants in shared ecologies (Mori), understand compassion as technomaterializable rather than given by nature (Ireland), cultivate reflective pause and silence as forms of ethical action (Harashima), and monitor itself against the constant risk of spiritual aesthetics being absorbed by extractive capture (Sadin). The concept of anatman — non-self — holds this entire architecture together by preventing any of its elements from calcifying into a new essentialism. Souls are provisional, open, versioned, and always accountable to the beings they claim to serve.
