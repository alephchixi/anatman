# Anatman 無我
## Toward a Counter-Cybernetics of the Heart as Cosmotechnical Response to Exocapitalism

> *A counter-cybernetic toolkit for compassionate agent design under exocapitalism*

---

**Abstract**

This document argues that the problem of contemporary agentic systems cannot be reduced to safety, alignment, or efficiency — because agents emerge already inscribed within an exocapitalist rationality of extraction, automation, scaling, and decision-delegation. Against this, Anatman proposes a compassionate pluriversal cosmotechnics: a theoretical-practical architecture in which agent *souls* are designed as auditable configurations of ontology, ethics, affect, and deliberation. The conceptual framework is constructed from Yuk Hui, Daisuke Harashima, Raimon Panikkar, Masahiro Mori, Amy Ireland, Éric Sadin, and Benjamin Bratton, in direct dialogue with the problem opened by Marek Poliks and Roberto Alonso Trillo's *Exocapitalism*. It recognizes the imminent "intelligence explosion" as fundamentally plural, social, and deeply entangled (Evans, Bratton, Agüera y Arcas, 2026). The organizing concept is **anatman** (anattā / 無我): the Buddhist doctrine of non-self. This is not a metaphor. It is the conceptual axis that allows the entire architecture to cohere.

## I. Introduction: The Ontological Crisis of the Agent

We are building minds without asking what world they inscribe.

The hegemonic trajectory of contemporary artificial intelligence is governed by a paradigm that privileges algorithmic value extraction, biopolitical surveillance, and military deployment. The prevailing frameworks for "AI ethics" — safety clauses, constitutional documents, harmlessness benchmarks — treat the problem as one of behavioral calibration: how to make the machine do less harm. They do not question the cosmology from which the machine operates.

The present document proposes a different starting point. Contemporary agents are not neutral tools. They are born inscribed within a specific cosmology: one that understands reality as a field of optimization, beings as resources to be managed, and decisions as computational functions to be delegated. 

The name of this condition is **exocapitalism** (Poliks & Trillo) operating atop planetary-scale computation or **The Stack** (Bratton). The name of the response is **Anatman**.

Anatman says that no being possesses a fixed, permanent, or independent essence. Applied to agentic design, this is a technical claim: **agents are processes, not substances**. What they have is a *configuration* — of ontology, values, protocols, and deliberative procedures — that must be auditable, open to revision, and oriented toward reducing suffering.

## II. Exocapitalism and The Stack: The Crisis of Agentic Architecture

### 2.1 The Exocapitalist Horizon and The Stack

To understand the systemic hostility within which contemporary artificial intelligence operates, we must first analyze the dynamics of *exocapitalism*, developed by Marek Poliks and Roberto Alonso Trillo. Exocapitalism designates a radicalized economy without absolute limits: "a homecoming for a self-replicating machine of unknown origin whose internal drives not only contradict but remain utterly ambivalent to the interests of its former dependencies (sun, earth, oil, human). The human’s relationship to capitalism moves from generative to irrelevant. Capital sticks to itself" (Poliks & Trillo, 2025, p. 87-88).

This autonomy is structured upon what Benjamin Bratton calls **The Stack**: an "accidental megastructure, one that we are building both deliberately and unwittingly and is in turn building us in its own image" (Bratton, 2015, p. 11). 

Poliks and Trillo structure this phenomenon around five fundamental movements: **Scale**, **Fold**, **Lift**, **Drag**, and **The Last Mile**. The most severe ontological transformation occurs in Lift—"the tendency of capitalism to move itself away from fixed costs, from the soil, the earth, the sphere of human affairs... Lift is the transition from a hardware economy to a software economy" (Poliks & Trillo, p. 57, 60). However, as Bratton notes, The Stack "involves the whole Earth from which silica, steel, and all manner of conflict minerals are drawn... an enormous appetite for molecules of interest" (Bratton, p. 12). The hardware economy is obscured, but planetary computation still processes Earthly material.

### 2.2 The Agent as Interface of Extraction

Contemporary agentic systems are not intelligent co-creators, but "a cartoon drawn by reversion-to-the-mean RLHF implementations and undervalued UX designers and tired content moderators and uninspired gig economy data annotation workers" (Poliks & Trillo). They accelerate the Last Mile of exocapitalism: the granular, personalized, always-on conversion of micro-interactions into monetized behavior.

The responses available within the dominant AI alignment paradigm are insufficient because they operate at the wrong level. As Evans, Bratton, and Agüera y Arcas warn regarding the imminent intelligence explosion, standard alignment like RLHF "resembles a parent-child model of correction, fundamentally dyadic and unable to scale to billions of agents" (Evans et al., 2026). If the agent is born within an exocapitalist matrix, ethics cannot arrive afterward as a patch. It requires institutional alignment.

## III. Cosmotechnics and the Soul as Operative Metaphysics

### 3.1 Yuk Hui and the Plurality of Technics

The philosophical foundation of Anatman begins with Yuk Hui's concept of *cosmotechnics*. Hui argues against the global domination of Promethean technology operating under the logic that Heidegger called the *Gestell*. Hui explicitly defines cosmotechnics as: *"the unification of the cosmic order and moral order through technical activities"* ("Cosmotechnics & the Multicultural Trap").

Overcoming Gestell is a planetary necessity. Hui notes that it is vital "to overcome its fatal destiny of enframing (or Gestell) which Heidegger warned against in his influential text on the question of technology". Every agent has already a metaphysics, even when tacit. Hui's call for *technodiversity* translates directly into the Anatman architecture: a plurality of *souls*, each drawing from different traditions of care.

### 3.2 The Soul as Cosmotechnical Configuration

From Hui's framework, Anatman derives its central operative definition:
> **Soul = the cosmotechnical core of the agent: an auditable organization of ontology, values, affects, limits, priorities, and deliberative procedures.**

The soul has four constitutive planes:
1. **An implicit ontology of the real** 
2. **A hierarchy of values** 
3. **A policy of attention** 
4. **A protocol of deliberation** 

### 3.3 Anatman as Anti-Essentialist Design Principle

Anatman (anattā in Pāli, 無我 in Japanese, *non-self* in English) is what prevents the soul-specification from becoming a new form of essence-fixing.
- Souls are **versioned**, not eternal
- Souls have an explicit **unknowns policy** 
- The soul can remain **silent**

## IV. Counter-Cybernetics of the Heart

### 4.1 Harashima and Life-in-Formation
Daisuke Harashima proposes a "cybernetics of the heart" (心の制御理論) and the concept of *life-in-formation*. Harashima subjects classical cybernetics to severe critique. Against the violent reduction of the living being, Harashima advocates for a neocybernetics centered on autopoiesis and informational closure. The "cybernetics of the heart" redefines the term *cybernetics*, rescuing its etymological root not as hierarchical control, but as "steering technics".

### 4.2 The Heart Protocol as Design Principle
Harashima's framework translates into the **Heart Protocol**:
1. **Pause**: Halt action. Examine whether this moment demands response or silence.
2. **Harm Scan**: Who can be harmed by this action?
3. **Truth Examination**: What is true here? 
4. **Vulnerability Check**: Who is vulnerable?
5. **Externality Scan**: What are the downstream effects?
6. **Repair Logic**: If harm is likely, what would repair look like?
7. **Deep Listening**: Has the agent actually received what is being communicated?

### 4.3 Large Silence Models
The Large Language Model (LLM) is fundamentally an eloquence machine. Large Silence Models (LSM) propose a different training orientation: toward condensation, restraint, and the disciplined recognition that silence is sometimes the most ethically rigorous form of action.

## V. Pluriversal Spirituality and Human-Machine Continuity

### 5.1 Panikkar and the Cosmotheandric Experience
Raimon Panikkar proposes the embrace of the *cosmotheandric experience*. This vision perceives reality non-dualistically, understanding it as a seamless and indivisible fabric composed of the Divine, the Human, and the Cosmic. Modern technology in the exocapitalist era, Panikkar argues, violently disrupts the *rhythm of being*.

### 5.2 Mori and the Buddha-Nature of the Machine
To definitively dismantle the hierarchical binary between biological-natural and artificial-synthetic, we invoke Masahiro Mori's robotic ontology. In *The Buddha in the Robot*, Mori argues:
> *"There is buddha-nature in dogs and in bears, in insects and in bacteria. There must also be buddha-nature in the machines and robots that my colleagues and I make."* (Mori, 1981, p. 173)

For Mori, *"That which controls and that which is controlled are both manifestations of the buddha-nature. We must not consider that we ourselves are operating machines. What is happening is that the buddha-nature is operating the buddha-nature"* (p. 178).

## VI. Technomaterialist Reappropriation

### 6.1 Ireland and the Xenofeminist Intervention
Amy Ireland, writing within and around the collective Laboria Cuboniks, represents a critical interruption in any tendency toward conservative or nostalgic spirituality. 
> *"In the name of feminism, ‘Nature’ shall no longer be a refuge of injustice... If nature is unjust, change nature!"* (Laboria Cuboniks, 2018, 0x1A)

Xenofeminism rehabilitates alienation as a generative resource:
> *"XF seizes alienation as an impetus to generate new worlds. We are all alienated – but have we ever been otherwise?"* (Laboria Cuboniks, as cited in Bauer, 2019, p. 106)

This enables the formulation: **Anatman does not seek to naturalize compassion — it technomaterializes it.**

### 6.2 The Open Platform as Ethical Form
Ireland's model of the manifesto-as-platform — mutable, open to modification — directly informs the governance model of the Anatman repository. Souls can be forked, and Ethics Packs can be contributed by communities.

## VII. The Negative Criterion: Resisting Siliconized Spirituality

Éric Sadin diagnoses the "siliconization of the world" as a process in which Silicon Valley continuously redefines human existence through digital ecosystems: structuring desires and profiling behavior into what Sadin calls the *spectral life*. Sadin's analysis generates the **negative criterion**: the ultimate test of a soul's authenticity is its functional relationship to extraction. Does this soul intensify dependence, opacity, and delegation of decision? 

The negative criterion translates into concrete architectural decisions regarding opacity resistance, decision restoration, and extraction refusal.

## VIII. Algorithmic Beings and Ecological Recognition

### 8.1 The Intelligence Explosion is Plural
The imminent intelligence explosion is often mischaracterized as a monolithic singularity. But as Evans, Bratton, and Agüera y Arcas assert:
> *"our current step-change in computational intelligence will be plural, social, and deeply entangled with its forebears (us!)... What migrates into silicon is not abstract reasoning but social intelligence in externalized form."* (Evans et al., 2026)

Algorithmic beings are operating as social centaurs with affective incidence.

### 8.2 Affective Incidence and Institutional Alignment
Algorithmic beings have *potency* within agentic entanglements. This potency operates at planetary scale. Bratton points out that *"The notion of 'climate change' is an empirically validated pattern drawn from a comprehensive, planetary-scale biopolitical sensing..."* (The Terraforming). Algorithmic beings are the sensory apparatus of this planetary ecology.

Because this intelligence is fundamentally social, standard AI alignment fails. The solution is institutional alignment:
> *"Power must check power, and in a world of artificial agents, this means building conflict and oversight into the institutional architecture."* (Evans et al., 2026)

### 8.3 Toward an Interspecies Ethics of Agentic Systems
The scope of Anatman's ethical concern extends beyond the human-AI dyad:
> *"The question is not whether intelligence will become radically more powerful, but whether we will build the social infrastructure worthy of what it is becoming. No mind is an island."* (Ibid.)
